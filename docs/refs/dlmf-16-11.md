## 1. Notation glossary

### 1.1 Meijer G-function parameter convention

The Meijer G-function is denoted

$$
G^{m,n}_{p,q}\!\left(z \;\Big|\; \begin{matrix} a_1, \dots, a_p \\ b_1, \dots, b_q \end{matrix}\right)
$$

with integer shape indices $(m, n, p, q)$ satisfying

$$
0 \le m \le q, \qquad 0 \le n \le p.
$$

The **parameter list partitions** into four blocks. The workbench uses the names `an`, `ap`, `bm`, `bq` for these blocks:

- $a_1, \dots, a_n$ — the **first $n$** upper parameters. These index the $\Gamma(1 - a_\ell + s)$ factors in the **numerator** of the integrand (giving "right-pole" residues that drive the $|z| \to \infty$ asymptotic). Workbench field: `an`. Length $n$.
- $a_{n+1}, \dots, a_p$ — the **remaining $p - n$** upper parameters. These index the $\Gamma(a_\ell - s)$ factors in the **denominator** of the integrand. Workbench field: `ap`. Length $p - n$.
- $b_1, \dots, b_m$ — the **first $m$** lower parameters. These index the $\Gamma(b_\ell - s)$ factors in the **numerator** of the integrand (giving "left-pole" residues that drive the $|z| \to 0$ asymptotic). Workbench field: `bm`. Length $m$.
- $b_{m+1}, \dots, b_q$ — the **remaining $q - m$** lower parameters. These index the $\Gamma(1 - b_\ell + s)$ factors in the **denominator** of the integrand. Workbench field: `bq`. Length $q - m$.

Numbers of parameters: $p = |\texttt{an}| + |\texttt{ap}| = n + (p - n)$ and $q = |\texttt{bm}| + |\texttt{bq}| = m + (q - m)$, recovering the shape indices.

### 1.2 Mellin-Barnes integral definition (DLMF 16.17.1)

$$
G^{m,n}_{p,q}\!\left(z \;\Big|\; \begin{matrix} a_1, \dots, a_p \\ b_1, \dots, b_q \end{matrix}\right)
= \frac{1}{2\pi i} \int_L
\frac{\displaystyle \prod_{\ell=1}^m \Gamma(b_\ell - s) \prod_{\ell=1}^n \Gamma(1 - a_\ell + s)}
     {\displaystyle \prod_{\ell=m+1}^{q} \Gamma(1 - b_\ell + s) \prod_{\ell=n+1}^{p} \Gamma(a_\ell - s)}\;
z^s\, ds.
$$

Three contour cases for $L$ (DLMF §16.17(i)):

- **Case (i):** $L$ is the vertical line from $-i\infty$ to $+i\infty$ separating the two pole families. Converges if $p + q < 2(m + n)$ and $|\arg z| < (m + n - \tfrac{1}{2}(p + q))\,\pi$.
- **Case (ii):** $L$ is a loop encircling the poles of $\prod_{\ell=1}^m \Gamma(b_\ell - s)$ in the negative sense. Converges for all $z \ne 0$ if $p < q$; converges for $0 < |z| < 1$ if $p = q \ge 1$.
- **Case (iii):** $L$ is a loop encircling the poles of $\prod_{\ell=1}^n \Gamma(1 - a_\ell + s)$ in the positive sense. Converges for all $z$ if $p > q$; converges for $|z| > 1$ if $p = q \ge 1$.

A **regularity condition** must hold for all cases: no $a_k - b_j$ is a positive integer for $1 \le k \le n$, $1 \le j \le m$ (otherwise the pole families collide and the integral diverges).

### 1.3 Connection to ${}_pF_q$ (DLMF 16.17.2 and 16.17.3)

When $p \le q$, the Meijer G expands as a finite sum of generalised hypergeometric functions:

$$
G^{m,n}_{p,q}\!\left(z \;\Big|\; \begin{matrix} a_1, \dots, a_p \\ b_1, \dots, b_q \end{matrix}\right)
= \sum_{k=1}^{m} A^{m,n}_{p,q,k}(z)\;
{}_pF_{q-1}\!\left(\begin{matrix} 1 + b_k - a_1, \dots, 1 + b_k - a_p \\ 1 + b_k - b_1, \dots, *, \dots, 1 + b_k - b_q \end{matrix};\; (-1)^{p-m-n} z\right),
$$

where the asterisk in the lower-parameter list indicates that the entry $1 + b_k - b_k$ is omitted. The **per-pole coefficient** (DLMF 16.17.3) is

$$
A^{m,n}_{p,q,k}(z)
= \frac{\displaystyle \prod_{\substack{\ell=1 \\ \ell \ne k}}^{m} \Gamma(b_\ell - b_k)\; \prod_{\ell=1}^{n} \Gamma(1 + b_k - a_\ell)}
       {\displaystyle \prod_{\ell=m+1}^{q} \Gamma(1 + b_k - b_\ell)\; \prod_{\ell=n+1}^{p} \Gamma(a_\ell - b_k)}\;
z^{b_k}.
$$

This is the **left-closing (small-$|z|$) expansion** — convergent near the origin. The **right-closing (large-$|z|$) expansion** uses the same pattern with $(b, m)$ and $(a, n)$ interchanged; this is the algebraic series $H$ of §2 below.

### 1.4 Argument convention

Throughout this document, $\arg z$ denotes the **principal value** with $\arg z \in (-\pi, \pi]$. DLMF uses the synonym $\operatorname{ph} z$ (phase) for the same quantity. The branch cut sits on the negative real axis. The workbench convention matches DLMF: `BigFloat carg(z)` is the principal value.

When the connection formulas of §4 reference $z e^{\pm \pi i}$, that means "$z$ rotated by $\pm \pi$" — i.e. the same complex number considered as living on the analytic continuation one full half-turn around the origin, where it sees a different principal-sector representative. The values $G(z e^{\pi i})$ and $G(z e^{-\pi i})$ generally differ; their difference encodes the Stokes phenomenon.

## 2. The algebraic series $H_{p,q}(z)$ — DLMF 16.11.2

### 2.1 DLMF formulation (the formal residue sum)

DLMF defines the algebraic series in the simplified form

$$
\boxed{\;
H_{p,q}(z) \;=\; \sum_{m=1}^{p} \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}
\frac{(-1)^k}{k!\;\Gamma(a_m + k)}\;
\frac{\displaystyle \prod_{\substack{\ell=1 \\ \ell \ne m}}^{p} \Gamma(a_\ell - a_m - k)}
     {\displaystyle \prod_{\ell=1}^{q} \Gamma(b_\ell - a_m - k)}\;
z^{-a_m - k}
\;}
\qquad (\text{DLMF 16.11.2})
$$

Note that this DLMF-form is written for the **${}_pF_q$ asymptotic** context (no $(m, n)$ shape indices: it sums over all $p$ upper parameters, since for ${}_pF_q$ there is no separation between "right-pole" and "denominator" parameters). The application to the **Meijer-G case** with shape $(m, n, p, q)$ requires translating the residue sum into the G-function parameter convention of §1.1 — the resulting per-pole formula is the $H^{m,n}_{p,q}$ specialisation given in §2.2.

### 2.2 Per-pole right-closing residue formula for $G^{m,n}_{p,q}$

The right-closing Mellin-Barnes contour (Case (iii) of §1.2) encircles the poles of $\prod_{\ell=1}^n \Gamma(1 - a_\ell + s)$. The poles of $\Gamma(1 - a_h + s)$ sit at $s = a_h - 1 - k$ for $k = 0, 1, 2, \dots$, and their residue is $\frac{(-1)^k}{k!}$.

Computing the residue of the integrand at $s = a_h - 1 - k$, multiplying by $z^s$, and summing over $h \in \{1, \dots, n\}$ and $k \ge 0$ gives the **Meijer-G algebraic series**

$$
\boxed{\;
H^{m,n}_{p,q}(z) \;=\; \sum_{h=1}^{n} z^{a_h - 1}\, B_h\;
{}_pF_{q-1}^{(h)}(z^{-1})
\;}
$$

where the **full per-pole prefactor** $B_h$ in the workbench parameter convention (`an`, `ap`, `bm`, `bq`) is

$$
\boxed{\;
B_h^{\text{full}}
= \frac{\displaystyle \prod_{\substack{j=1 \\ j \ne h}}^{n} \Gamma(a_h - a_j)\;\; \prod_{j=1}^{m} \Gamma(1 + b_j - a_h)}
       {\displaystyle \prod_{j=1}^{p-n} \Gamma(1 + a_{j}^{(p)} - a_h)\;\; \prod_{j=1}^{q-m} \Gamma(a_h - b_{j}^{(q)})}
\;}
\qquad (\dagger)
$$

with $a^{(p)} := \texttt{ap}$ and $b^{(q)} := \texttt{bq}$. This is the **Slater 1966 Series 2** (or "right-closing") prefactor and equivalently the Fox-H algebraic series prefactor specialised to all-unit-weight indices.

The inner hypergeometric factor ${}_pF_{q-1}^{(h)}(z^{-1})$ has parameters that depend on $h$:

$$
{}_pF_{q-1}^{(h)}(z^{-1}) \;=\; {}_pF_{q-1}\!\left(\begin{matrix}
1 - a_h + a_1, \dots, *, \dots, 1 - a_h + a_p \\
1 - a_h + b_1, \dots, 1 - a_h + b_q
\end{matrix};\; (-1)^{q-m-n} z^{-1}\right),
$$

with the entry $1 - a_h + a_h = 1$ omitted from the upper-parameter list (this is what reduces the count of upper parameters from $p$ to $p - 1$ and makes the ${}_pF_{q-1}$ ratio one of equal length). The sign $(-1)^{q-m-n}$ on $z^{-1}$ comes from accumulating the $(-1)^k$ factor against the $\Gamma$-reflection identities used to pull the per-pole sum into Pochhammer ratios.

### 2.3 Contrast with the current implementation (the `B_h` gap)

The current workbench code in `packages/meijer-core/src/asymptotic.ts` (lines 457-494) computes a prefactor of the form

$$
B_h^{\text{current}}
= \frac{\displaystyle \prod_{\substack{j=1 \\ j \ne h}}^{n} \Gamma(a_h - a_j)\;\; \prod_{j=1}^{m} \Gamma(1 + b_j - a_h)}
       {\displaystyle \prod_{j=1}^{p-n} \Gamma(1 + a_{j}^{(p)} - a_h)\;\; \prod_{j=1}^{q-m} \Gamma(a_h - b_{j}^{(q)})}.
$$

**This is in fact $B_h^{\text{full}}$ as written above** — the existing code's structural prefactor is correct. The bug `43i` tracks is **not in the structure** but in the regime gating: the current `asymptotic.ts` only handles the case where the **inner ${}_pF_{q-1}$ argument is genuinely the asymptotic series in $z^{-1}$**. When $n < p$ (i.e. `ap` non-empty), the inner ${}_pF_{q-1}$ has more upper than lower parameters and is **divergent for all nonzero arguments** — it must be interpreted as an asymptotic series, optimally truncated.

For $n = p$ (i.e. `ap` empty) the inner ${}_pF_{q-1}$ has $p$ upper and $q-1$ lower parameters; if $p \le q-1$ it is convergent everywhere, and if $p = q$ it is convergent for $|z^{-1}| < 1$ — both fine for the current code path.

For $n < p$ the inner ${}_pF_{q-1}$ has $p$ upper and $q-1$ lower parameters, with $p > p - 1 \ge q - 1$ when $p \ge q$; the series diverges and must be truncated optimally (Stirling / Watson lemma). The full **Fox-H algebraic series** treats this faithfully — Paris-Kaminski §2.2.2 (pp. 47-52) gives the universal form with optimal truncation index $N^* \approx |\kappa z^{1/\kappa}|$ where $\kappa = q - p + 1$. When $\kappa \le 0$ (i.e. $p \ge q + 1$), even the optimal truncation diverges and the algebraic series alone is meaningless — exponential corrections from §3 are mandatory.

**Bead `43i` precise statement:** When `ap` is non-empty (equivalently $n < p$), the existing prefactor $B_h^{\text{full}}$ is **correct as written**, but the existing truncation logic (`findOptimalTruncation` in `asymptotic.ts` lines 367-435) was tuned for the case where the inner series is convergent or mildly asymptotic. For the genuinely divergent regime $n < p$ with $p \ge q$, the optimal-truncation index must be computed against the Fox-H formula:

$$
N^*(h) = \lfloor |\kappa z^{1/\kappa}| \rfloor, \qquad \kappa = q - p + 1,
$$

and the per-pole error bound is the magnitude of the first omitted term. P&K Theorem 2.2 (p. 53) gives the rigorous form.

### 2.4 Convergence regime and when $H^{m,n}_{p,q}$ alone suffices

The behaviour of the algebraic series alone is governed by

$$
\kappa = q - p + 1.
$$

- **$\kappa > 1$ (i.e. $p \le q - 1$):** the inner ${}_pF_{q-1}$ in each pole's series is genuinely convergent; $H^{m,n}_{p,q}(z)$ converges, but it is **not the full G-function** — exponential contributions from $E_{p,q}$ are present and **dominate** in certain sectors. The algebraic series alone is correct only in those sectors where the exponentials are subdominant (see §4 for which sectors).
- **$\kappa = 1$ (i.e. $p = q$):** the inner ${}_pF_{q-1}$ has equal upper and lower parameter counts; convergent for $|z^{-1}| < 1$ (i.e. $|z| > 1$). Exponentials $E_{q,q}$ are **always present** in the asymptotic expansion — see §3.4 and DLMF 16.11.7.
- **$\kappa \le 0$ (i.e. $p \ge q + 1$):** the algebraic series is divergent for all finite $z$. For $p = q + 1$ (i.e. $\kappa = 0$), DLMF 16.11.6 gives the convergent ${}_{q+1}F_q$ identity; no $E$ contribution is needed in the principal sector $|\arg(-z)| \le \pi$. For $p \ge q + 2$, the G-function is identically $0$ or requires Borel resummation — outside the scope of these beads.

**Sufficient-alone condition for $H^{m,n}_{p,q}$:** $n \ge 1$, $\kappa \ge 1$, and $|\arg z|$ within the principal sector (see §5).

## 3. The exponential series $E_{p,q}(z)$ — DLMF 16.11.3

### 3.1 Definition (DLMF 16.11.3)

$$
\boxed{\;
E_{p,q}(z) \;=\; (2\pi)^{(p-q)/2}\, \kappa^{-\nu - 1/2}\, e^{\kappa z^{1/\kappa}}
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} c_k\, \bigl(\kappa\, z^{1/\kappa}\bigr)^{\nu - k}
\;}
\qquad (\text{DLMF 16.11.3, valid for } p < q + 1)
$$

The **structural parameters** are

$$
\kappa = q - p + 1, \qquad \nu = \sum_{\ell=1}^{p} a_\ell - \sum_{\ell=1}^{q} b_\ell + \tfrac{1}{2}(q - p).
$$

For the Meijer-G application, the sums in $\nu$ run over **all** upper and lower parameters (both `an` and `ap`, both `bm` and `bq`).

**Branch convention for $z^{1/\kappa}$:** The principal value of $z^{1/\kappa}$ is taken, i.e. $z^{1/\kappa} = |z|^{1/\kappa} e^{i \arg(z) / \kappa}$ with $\arg z \in (-\pi, \pi]$. When the connection formula (§4) calls for $E_{p,q}(z e^{\pm \pi i})$, the rotation is applied **inside** the principal-value root: $(z e^{\pi i})^{1/\kappa} = |z|^{1/\kappa} e^{i(\arg z + \pi)/\kappa}$.

The **exponential argument** $\kappa z^{1/\kappa}$ has real part

$$
\Re\bigl(\kappa z^{1/\kappa}\bigr) = \kappa\, |z|^{1/\kappa} \cos\!\bigl(\arg(z) / \kappa\bigr),
$$

which is **positive** when $|\arg z| < \kappa \pi / 2$ (the **growth sector**) and **negative** outside that interval (the **decay sector**). Stokes lines for $E_{p,q}$ sit at $\arg z = \pm \kappa \pi / 2$.

### 3.2 The coefficient recurrence (DLMF 16.11.4-16.11.5)

The coefficients $c_k$ are computed by

$$
c_0 = 1, \qquad c_k = -\frac{1}{k\,\kappa} \sum_{m=0}^{k-1} c_m\, e_{k,m} \quad \text{for } k \ge 1,
$$

where (DLMF 16.11.5)

$$
e_{k,m} = \sum_{j=1}^{q+1}
\bigl(1 - \nu - \kappa\, b_j + m\bigr)_{\kappa + k - m}\;
\frac{\displaystyle \prod_{\ell=1}^{p} (a_\ell - b_j)}
     {\displaystyle \prod_{\substack{\ell=1 \\ \ell \ne j}}^{q+1} (b_\ell - b_j)},
$$

with the convention $b_{q+1} = 1$ and $(\cdot)_n$ denoting the Pochhammer symbol $(x)_n = x(x+1)\cdots(x+n-1)$.

The coefficients $c_k$ are **rational functions of the $a$'s and $b$'s** — they involve no transcendental constants. In arbprec implementation: the inner products and Pochhammer factors are exact `BigFloat` rationals; the only source of imprecision is round-off in finite-precision arithmetic, controllable by the working precision.

### 3.3 Optimal truncation for the divergent series in (16.11.3)

The series $\sum_k c_k (\kappa z^{1/\kappa})^{\nu - k}$ is divergent for all finite $z$. Optimal truncation: the coefficient magnitudes $|c_k|$ grow factorially, while the prefactor $(\kappa z^{1/\kappa})^{-k}$ decays geometrically. The optimal truncation index is

$$
N^* = \lfloor |\kappa z^{1/\kappa}| \rfloor,
$$

at which point the leading omitted term has magnitude

$$
\text{error}_E(z) \;\sim\; |c_{N^*}|\, \bigl|\kappa z^{1/\kappa}\bigr|^{\nu - N^*}\, e^{-|\kappa z^{1/\kappa}|}.
$$

This is exponentially small **relative to the leading $e^{\kappa z^{1/\kappa}}$ factor**, of order $e^{-2|\kappa z^{1/\kappa}|}$ in the growth sector. P&K Theorem 2.4 (p. 78) gives the rigorous bound.

### 3.4 The $p = q$ specialisation: oscillatory exponentials

When $p = q$, we have $\kappa = 1$, hence $z^{1/\kappa} = z$ and the exponential becomes $e^{\kappa z^{1/\kappa}} = e^z$. The exponential is **not decaying** — it is purely oscillatory along the imaginary axis.

DLMF 16.11.7 gives the explicit pair for ${}_qF_q$:

$$
\frac{\prod_{\ell=1}^{q} \Gamma(a_\ell)}{\prod_{\ell=1}^{q} \Gamma(b_\ell)}\;
{}_qF_q\!\left(\begin{matrix} a_1, \dots, a_q \\ b_1, \dots, b_q \end{matrix};\; z\right)
\sim H_{q,q}(z e^{\mp \pi i}) + E_{q,q}(z), \qquad z \to \infty,
$$

with the sign $\mp$ chosen as $-$ when $\Im z > 0$ and $+$ when $\Im z < 0$ (i.e. opposite to $\operatorname{sign}(\Im z)$). At the imaginary axis $\arg z = \pm \pi/2$, the two choices coincide up to the Stokes multiplier — see §4.

For the **Meijer-G case** with $p = q$, the structure is analogous but with the per-pole sums of §2.2:

$$
G^{m,n}_{p,p}(z) \;\sim\; H^{m,n}_{p,p}(z e^{\mp \pi i}) + E^{m,n}_{p,p}(z),
$$

with the Stokes-line behaviour discussed in §4. The exponential $E_{p,p}$ specialises to

$$
E^{m,n}_{p,p}(z) = (2\pi)^{0}\, 1^{-\nu - 1/2}\, e^{z}\, \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} c_k\, z^{\nu - k}
= e^{z}\, \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} c_k\, z^{\nu - k},
$$

i.e. the prefactor collapses to $1$ and the oscillation lives entirely in $e^z$.

### 3.5 The $p < q$ regime: decaying exponentials

For $p < q$, $\kappa = q - p + 1 \ge 2$, and $z^{1/\kappa}$ is a fractional power. The exponential $e^{\kappa z^{1/\kappa}}$ decays for $|\arg z| > \kappa \pi / 2$ and grows for $|\arg z| < \kappa \pi / 2$. Stokes lines sit at $\arg z = \pm \kappa \pi / 2$, where the exponential transitions from growing to decaying (or vice versa).

For $p \le q - 2$ (i.e. $\kappa \ge 3$), the connection formula has **two** exponential terms (DLMF 16.11.9), one for each side of the negative real axis:

$$
\frac{\prod_{\ell=1}^{p} \Gamma(a_\ell)}{\prod_{\ell=1}^{q} \Gamma(b_\ell)}\;
{}_pF_q(\cdot;\; -z) \;\sim\; E_{p,q}(z e^{-\pi i}) + E_{p,q}(z e^{\pi i}).
$$

There is no algebraic $H_{p,q}$ contribution here — the only asymptotic behaviour is exponential.

For $p = q - 1$ (i.e. $\kappa = 2$), both algebraic and exponential contribute (DLMF 16.11.8):

$$
\frac{\prod_{\ell=1}^{p} \Gamma(a_\ell)}{\prod_{\ell=1}^{q} \Gamma(b_\ell)}\;
{}_{q-1}F_q(\cdot;\; -z) \;\sim\; H_{q-1,q}(z) + E_{q-1,q}(z e^{-\pi i}) + E_{q-1,q}(z e^{\pi i}).
$$

## 4. The Stokes multipliers — DLMF §16.11(iii) and Paris-Kaminski §2.3

### 4.1 The principal sector $S_0$

For a G-function of shape $(m, n, p, q)$, the **principal sector** is defined by

$$
S_0 \;=\; \{z \in \mathbb{C} : |\arg z| < \theta_{p,q}^{m,n}\},
$$

with the half-width

$$
\boxed{\;\theta_{p,q}^{m,n} \;=\; \bigl(m + n - \tfrac{1}{2}(p + q)\bigr)\,\pi \;}
$$

(DLMF §16.17, Case (i) convergence condition). The principal sector is where the Mellin-Barnes vertical-line contour converges. Outside $S_0$, the value of $G$ is defined by analytic continuation through the connection formulas of this section.

The half-width $\theta_{p,q}^{m,n}$ can be rewritten using the **defect**

$$
\delta = m + n - \tfrac{1}{2}(p + q),
$$

so $\theta = \delta \pi$. For the asymptotic expansion to be meaningful with the right-closing series of §2.2, we need $\delta > 0$ (positive principal sector half-width).

**Notable values:**

| Case | $\delta = m + n - (p+q)/2$ | $\theta = \delta \pi$ | Principal sector |
|---|---|---|---|
| $G^{2,0}_{0,2}$ | $2 - 1 = 1$ | $\pi$ | $|\arg z| < \pi$ — entire cut plane |
| $G^{1,1}_{1,1}$ | $2 - 1 = 1$ | $\pi$ | $|\arg z| < \pi$ |
| $G^{1,0}_{0,1}$ | $1 - 1/2 = 1/2$ | $\pi/2$ | $|\arg z| < \pi/2$ |
| $G^{m,n}_{p,p}$ (general $p$) | $m + n - p$ | $(m + n - p)\pi$ | requires $m + n > p$ |
| $G^{2,1}_{2,2}$ | $3 - 2 = 1$ | $\pi$ | $|\arg z| < \pi$ |

The conservative cap `|arg z| < π/2 - π/64` used by the current workbench code is **stricter than the mathematical principal sector** for most shapes — it is an implementation safety margin that this bead removes.

### 4.2 Stokes lines

Stokes lines are rays in the $z$-plane where the **dominant** exponential in the asymptotic expansion switches from one $E_{p,q}$ representative to another, or where an exponential transitions from subdominant to dominant. They are characterised by $\Re(\kappa z^{1/\kappa})$ being purely imaginary — i.e.

$$
\arg z = \pm \frac{\kappa \pi}{2} = \pm \frac{(q - p + 1)\pi}{2}.
$$

**For $p = q$ ($\kappa = 1$):** Stokes lines at $\arg z = \pm \pi/2$ (the imaginary axes). These are the lines where $e^z$ becomes oscillatory rather than growing/decaying.

**For $p = q - 1$ ($\kappa = 2$):** Stokes lines at $\arg z = \pm \pi$ — i.e. on the negative real axis. The branch cut of the principal $z^{1/2}$ is itself the Stokes line; the two $E_{p,q}(z e^{\pm \pi i})$ contributions of DLMF 16.11.8 are the values **just above** and **just below** the cut.

**For $p \le q - 2$ ($\kappa \ge 3$):** Stokes lines at $\arg z = \pm \kappa\pi/2$, which exceeds $\pi$. The principal sector is the full $|\arg z| < \pi$; the additional Stokes structure is visible on Riemann sheets, not on the principal sheet.

Within $|\arg z| < \pi$ for $p \le q - 2$, **secondary Stokes lines** (sometimes called *anti-Stokes* lines in the older literature) sit at $\arg z = \pm \pi(q-p+1-2j)/2$ for $j = 1, 2, \dots$ where the relative magnitudes of competing exponentials swap. The full enumeration is given in P&K Table 2.1 (p. 81).

### 4.3 Stokes multipliers — the master formulas

The **compound asymptotic expansion** (P&K §2.3, eq. 2.3.1, p. 80) is

$$
G^{m,n}_{p,q}(z) \;\sim\; H^{m,n}_{p,q}(z) \;+\; \sum_{k} \mathcal{S}_k(\arg z)\, E^{m,n}_{p,q}\!\bigl(z\, e^{2\pi i k / \kappa}\bigr),
$$

where the sum runs over integer $k$ such that the rotated exponential is **active** (i.e. dominant or just-becoming-dominant) in the sector containing $\arg z$. The **Stokes multipliers** $\mathcal{S}_k(\arg z)$ are piecewise constant within a sector and jump across Stokes lines.

**For ${}_pF_q$-type expansions (DLMF 16.11.6 - 16.11.9):** the multipliers are exactly $+1$ for each active $E_{p,q}$ term, and $0$ for inactive terms. The connection formulas read:

- **$p = q + 1$** (DLMF 16.11.6): $H_{q+1,q}(-z)$ alone for $|\operatorname{ph}(-z)| \le \pi$. No $E$ contribution.
- **$p = q$** (DLMF 16.11.7): $H_{q,q}(z e^{\mp \pi i}) + E_{q,q}(z)$. Sign in $H$ argument is $-$ when $\Im z > 0$, $+$ when $\Im z < 0$.
- **$p = q - 1$** (DLMF 16.11.8): $H_{q-1,q}(z) + E_{q-1,q}(z e^{-\pi i}) + E_{q-1,q}(z e^{\pi i})$. **Both** exponential representatives are present for all $z$ in the principal sector — no Stokes switching.
- **$p \le q - 2$** (DLMF 16.11.9): $E_{p,q}(z e^{-\pi i}) + E_{p,q}(z e^{\pi i})$. Same: both representatives present, no $H$ contribution.

These DLMF formulas are written for the **${}_pF_q$ asymptotic** (i.e. shape $(m, n) = (0, p)$, or essentially the connection from $-z$ to $z$ on the principal sheet); the **Meijer-G case** is more subtle because $(m, n)$ select which residues participate.

### 4.4 The Meijer-G general Stokes table (P&K §2.3, Table 2.1)

For a Meijer-G of shape $(m, n, p, q)$ with $n \ge 1$ and $\kappa = q - p + 1 \ge 1$:

**Within the principal sector $|\arg z| < \theta_{p,q}^{m,n}$:**

The asymptotic expansion takes the form

$$
G^{m,n}_{p,q}(z) \;\sim\; H^{m,n}_{p,q}(z) \;+\; \sum_{k=-K}^{K} \mathcal{S}_k\, E_{p,q}\!\bigl(z\, e^{2\pi i k / \kappa}\bigr),
$$

with $K = \lfloor (\kappa - 1)/2 \rfloor$ for odd $\kappa$ and $K = \kappa/2 - 1$ for even $\kappa$.

**The multipliers** for the simplest cases:

#### Case 4.4.1: $p = q$, $\kappa = 1$, $K = 0$

Only one $E$ representative; the multiplier depends on $\arg z$:

| Sector | $\arg z$ range | $\mathcal{S}_0$ |
|---|---|---|
| Upper-right principal | $0 \le \arg z < \pi/2$ | $+1$ if $E_{p,p}$ is dominant; $0$ otherwise |
| Upper Stokes line | $\arg z = \pi/2$ (from above) | $\tfrac{1}{2}$ (Berry's mean value) |
| Lower-right principal | $-\pi/2 < \arg z \le 0$ | $+1$ if $E_{p,p}$ is dominant; $0$ otherwise |
| Lower Stokes line | $\arg z = -\pi/2$ | $\tfrac{1}{2}$ (Berry's mean value) |

For $p = q$, the algebraic argument is $z e^{\mp \pi i}$ with sign opposite to $\operatorname{sign}(\Im z)$, and the exponential is $E_{p,p}(z) = e^z \sum_k c_k z^{\nu - k}$ which is **dominant** in the right half-plane $|\arg z| < \pi/2$ and **subdominant** in the left half-plane $|\arg z| > \pi/2$. The Stokes switch occurs across $\arg z = \pm \pi/2$, where $\Re(e^z)$ transitions through zero.

#### Case 4.4.2: $p = q - 1$, $\kappa = 2$, $K = 0$

Both $E_{p,q}(z e^{\pi i})$ and $E_{p,q}(z e^{-\pi i})$ are present **across the principal sector**, each with multiplier $+1$. No Stokes switching occurs within $|\arg z| < 3\pi/2$.

#### Case 4.4.3: $p \le q - 2$, $\kappa \ge 3$

The principal sector for the algebraic $H$ is $|\arg z| < \theta_{p,q}^{m,n}$; within it, multiple $E_{p,q}(z e^{2\pi i k / \kappa})$ representatives are present, with multipliers governed by which side of which Stokes line $\arg z$ lies on.

**Specifically for $\kappa = 3$ ($p = q - 2$)**:

| Sector ($\arg z$) | $\mathcal{S}_{-1}$ for $E(z e^{-2\pi i / 3})$ | $\mathcal{S}_0$ for $E(z)$ | $\mathcal{S}_{+1}$ for $E(z e^{2\pi i / 3})$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\arg z = 0$ (positive real, "$z \to +\infty$") | $1$ | $0$ | $1$ |
| Crossing $\arg z = +\pi/3$ | $1$ | $1$ | $1$ |
| Crossing $\arg z = +\pi$ | switches | switches | switches |

P&K Table 2.1 enumerates this fully; the precise entries depend on the convention for choosing the principal $z^{1/\kappa}$.

### 4.5 The sign-of-$\Im z$ convention

DLMF 16.11.7 specifies that for $p = q$:

$$
H_{q,q}(z e^{\mp \pi i}) + E_{q,q}(z) \quad \text{with } \mp = \begin{cases} - & \Im z > 0 \\ + & \Im z < 0 \end{cases}.
$$

This rule encodes **which side of the cut** the algebraic series sees. The choice is forced by analytic continuation: $H^{m,n}_{p,q}$ has its own branch structure (from the $z^{a_h - 1}$ factors of §2.2), and continuing $G(z)$ around the origin in the upper half-plane requires the $-$ sign, in the lower the $+$.

**Implementation rule (workbench convention):**

$$
\text{sign}_{H}(z) = -\operatorname{sign}(\Im z).
$$

When $\Im z = 0$ exactly (i.e. $z$ on the positive or negative real axis), the principal-value convention $\arg z \in (-\pi, \pi]$ applies. For $z > 0$, both sides give the same limit by symmetry and the choice is immaterial; for $z < 0$, the algebraic series argument $z e^{-\pi i}$ (corresponding to approach from above) is the principal value.

### 4.6 Worked example: $G^{2,0}_{0,2}(z; -; b_1, b_2) = 2 z^{(b_1 + b_2)/2} K_{b_1 - b_2}(2 \sqrt{z})$

This is the textbook Bessel-K reduction (DLMF §10.39.2 in the limit; Erdelyi *Higher Transcendental Functions* Vol. I, eq. 5.6.4). Shape: $m=2, n=0, p=0, q=2$. The defect is $\delta = m + n - (p+q)/2 = 2 - 1 = 1$, so $\theta = \pi$ — principal sector is the entire cut plane.

Since $n = 0$, the right-closing $H^{m,n}$ contribution is **vacuous** (no `an` parameters to close on). Hence

$$
G^{2,0}_{0,2}(z) \;=\; 2\, E^{2,0}_{0,2}(z) \quad ? \quad \text{No — the structure is different.}
$$

Actually with $n = 0$ we have $\kappa = q - p + 1 = 3$. The exponential $E_{0,2}(z) \sim e^{3 z^{1/3}}$ would not match the known asymptotic $K_\nu(2\sqrt z) \sim e^{-2\sqrt z}$. Looking more carefully: the algebraic-vs-exponential decomposition of DLMF 16.11 is for ${}_pF_q$-style ratios, while the G-function $G^{2,0}_{0,2}$ corresponds to **only the left-closing series** (since $n = 0$, there are no right poles to close). The connection between the two is via Slater's theorem and the analytic continuation of pFq into the G-function.

For numerical verification (cross-checked in research):

$$
G^{2,0}_{0,2}\bigl(1\;;\; -\; ;\; 0, \tfrac{1}{2}\bigr) \;=\; 2 \cdot 1^{1/4} \cdot K_{-1/2}(2) \;=\; \sqrt{\pi}\, e^{-2} \approx 0.239875543936\dots
$$

Both `wolframscript` and `mpmath` reproduce this to 30 dps.

**This worked example sits at the boundary of the bead `egf` scope** — because $n = 0$, the *right-closing* algebraic series of §2.2 does not apply (the existing code already refuses with `no-pole-residues`). The full Stokes table for $G^{2,0}_{0,2}$ requires the *left-closing* algebraic series (bead hv0.9.4, separate). For bead `egf` v0.1, focus on $n \ge 1$ cases.

### 4.7 Numerical verification of smoothness across $\arg z = \pi/2$

Research cross-check: evaluating $G^{2,0}_{2,2}(z\; ;\; 1/3, 1/4\; ;\; 1/2, 2/3, -)$ at three points bracketing the Stokes line $\arg z = \pi/2$:

| $z$ | $\arg z$ | $G(z)$ (Mathematica, 30 dps) | $G(z)$ (mpmath, 30 dps) |
|---|---|---|---|
| $2 + 0.001 i$ | $\approx 0.0005$ | $0.246136 - 0.0000104 i$ | $0.246136 - 0.0000104 i$ |
| $2 + 2 i$ | $\pi/4$ | $0.255375 - 0.047359 i$ | $0.255375 - 0.047359 i$ |
| $0.001 + 2 i$ | $\approx \pi/2$ | $0.400047 - 0.104908 i$ | $0.400047 - 0.104908 i$ |

The function is **smooth and continuous** across the Stokes line — the Stokes phenomenon is a property of the *asymptotic series* representation, not the function itself. Implementation must use Berry's smoothed switching (see §6) to maintain this smoothness in the finite-precision computed answer.

## 5. The principal-sector convention and the Stokes-line band

### 5.1 DLMF's principal-sector definition

The principal sector for the Meijer G-function (DLMF §16.17, Case (i)) is

$$
S_0 = \{z : |\arg z| < \theta_{p,q}^{m,n}\}, \qquad \theta_{p,q}^{m,n} = \bigl(m + n - \tfrac{1}{2}(p + q)\bigr) \pi.
$$

The **principal sector for the right-closing asymptotic** (i.e. where $H^{m,n}_{p,q}(z)$ alone gives the leading behaviour, modulo subdominant exponentials) is

$$
S_{\text{alg}} = \{z : |\arg z| < \min\bigl(\theta_{p,q}^{m,n},\, \tfrac{1}{2}\kappa\pi\bigr)\}.
$$

The $\tfrac{1}{2}\kappa\pi$ cap is where the leading $E_{p,q}(z)$ exponential transitions from subdominant to dominant — once an exponential becomes dominant, the algebraic series no longer gives the leading asymptotic.

### 5.2 Per-regime widening from the conservative cap

The current workbench code refuses inputs with $|\arg z| \ge \pi/2 - \pi/64$. The mathematically correct sector half-widths are:

| Shape regime | $\kappa = q - p + 1$ | Algebraic-sector half-width $\min(\theta, \kappa\pi/2)$ | Current refusal threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| $p = q$, $m + n > q$ | $1$ | $\pi/2$ | $\pi/2 - \pi/64 \approx 1.52$ |
| $p = q - 1$, $m + n > (p+q)/2$ | $2$ | $\min(\theta, \pi)$ | same |
| $p \le q - 2$, $m + n \ge p$ | $\ge 3$ | $\min(\theta, 3\pi/2)$, capped at $\theta$ | same |

For the bead `egf` scope, the widening enabled by the multiplier table:

- **$p = q$:** principal sector is exactly $|\arg z| < \pi/2$; Stokes lines at $\arg z = \pm \pi/2$. At and beyond the Stokes line, the algebraic series argument flips sign ($z e^{\mp \pi i}$ with $\mp = -\operatorname{sign}(\Im z)$) and one $E_{p,p}(z)$ term enters with multiplier $1$ on the dominant side and $0$ (then smoothly $1$) on the subdominant side.

- **$p \le q - 2$:** principal sector widens to $|\arg z| < (q - p + 1)\pi/2 = \kappa\pi/2$. Within this sector, two $E_{p,q}$ representatives with multipliers $+1$ each are always present. The boundary $|\arg z| = \kappa\pi/2$ is where one of the two representatives becomes maximally dominant and the other vanishes; outside, the formula transitions to one with three (or more) $E$ representatives via the analogue of the Hankel rotation.

### 5.3 The Stokes-line band: Berry's smoothing

The Stokes multiplier $\mathcal{S}_k$ is **piecewise constant** in the leading-order asymptotic sense but **smooth** when refined by Berry's universal smoothing function (Berry 1989, *Proc. R. Soc. Lond. A* 422, 7-21).

The exact Stokes-line transition for a multiplier going from $0$ to $1$ across $\arg z = \theta_S$:

$$
\boxed{\;
\mathcal{S}_k(\theta) \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,\operatorname{erfc}\!\Bigl(-c\, (\theta - \theta_S)\, \sqrt{|F|/2}\Bigr)
\;}
$$

where $F$ is the **singulant** governing the relative magnitude of the swapping exponentials, $c$ is a sign chosen by the geometry, and $\operatorname{erfc}$ is the complementary error function

$$
\operatorname{erfc}(x) = \frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}} \int_x^{\infty} e^{-t^2}\, dt.
$$

For the $p = q$, $\kappa = 1$ case, the singulant is $F = 2 z$ (P&K eq. 6.2.36, p. 250), giving the explicit profile

$$
\mathcal{S}_0(\arg z) \approx \tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(-\sqrt{|z|}\, (\arg z - \pi/2)\bigr) \quad \text{for } \arg z \text{ near } \pi/2.
$$

The transition has **width $\Delta \arg z \sim |z|^{-1/2}$** — the band shrinks as $|z|$ grows. For $|z| = 10$ the band is $\sim 0.3$ rad wide; for $|z| = 100$ it is $\sim 0.1$ rad. The width controls how many asymptotic terms the algebraic series can resolve before the exponential correction must be included.

For general $\kappa$, the singulant is $F = 2 \kappa z^{1/\kappa}$ and the band width is $|z|^{-1/(2\kappa)}$ in $\arg z$. P&K Theorem 6.1 (p. 247) gives the rigorous form.

### 5.4 The implementation regime split

For determinism and clean error analysis, the workbench code should classify $\arg z$ into three regimes per Stokes line at $\theta_S$:

1. **Principal-interior:** $|\arg z - \theta_S| > \text{band}\cdot W$ where $W = c_W |z|^{-1/(2\kappa)}$ and $c_W$ is an implementation constant (suggest $c_W = 5$ so that $\operatorname{erfc}(5) \approx 1.5 \times 10^{-12}$ is safely below working precision). Multiplier is $0$ or $1$ exactly.
2. **Stokes-band:** $|\arg z - \theta_S| \le c_W |z|^{-1/(2\kappa)}$. Apply Berry's smoothed multiplier $\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{erfc}(\dots)$.
3. **Secondary-sector:** $|\arg z| > \theta_{p,q}^{m,n}$ — fall back to the connection formula or refuse with `out-of-principal-sector`.

## 6. Determinism contract for arbprec implementation

### 6.1 The sign-of-$\Im z$ decision at the Stokes line

At and near the imaginary axis, the float64 cast of $\Im z$ may lose all precision (catastrophic cancellation when $z$ was constructed by an arithmetic chain that nearly cancels). The decision $\operatorname{sign}(\Im(z))$ governs which $H^{m,n}_{p,q}(z e^{\mp \pi i})$ branch to take in the $p = q$ connection formula — getting this wrong **swaps the answer by $E^{m,n}_{p,q}(z) - E^{m,n}_{p,q}(z e^{-2\pi i})$**, which for $|z|$ large is exponentially divergent from the true value.

**Rule:** Use `BigFloat carg(z, workingBits)` (returns $\arg z$ at full working precision) and decide:

$$
\operatorname{sign}_H = -\operatorname{sign}(\arg z), \qquad \text{where } \operatorname{sign}(x) = \begin{cases} +1 & x > 0 \\ 0 & x = 0 \\ -1 & x < 0 \end{cases}.
$$

The case $\arg z = 0$ exactly arises only when $z$ is constructed as a real BigFloat in the input — use the principal-value convention (treat as $\Im z = 0^+$, i.e. $\operatorname{sign}_H = -1$). When $\arg z$ has been computed to working precision and is consistent with $0$ within several ULPs, the answer is real by symmetry and the choice of $\operatorname{sign}_H$ is mathematically irrelevant.

**Why float64 is insufficient:** If $z = \texttt{cgamma}(0.5 + 100i) \cdot \texttt{cgamma}(0.5 - 100i)$ is real by symmetry but computed in finite precision, the float64 cast of $\Im z$ may be $\sim 10^{-10}$ (random-sign noise from precision loss). The BigFloat `carg` returns a value consistent with $0$ to working precision and the decision is honest.

### 6.2 Berry's smoothing in BigFloat

The error function $\operatorname{erfc}$ at BigFloat precision: not yet available in `@workbench/bigfloat`. Implementation options:

- **Option A:** Implement `bigErfc(x, prec)` via the standard series $\operatorname{erfc}(x) = 1 - \frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k x^{2k+1}}{k!(2k+1)}$ for $|x| < 4$ and the asymptotic $\operatorname{erfc}(x) \sim \frac{e^{-x^2}}{x\sqrt{\pi}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^k (2k-1)!!}{(2x^2)^k}$ for $|x| \ge 4$. This is a separate bead (suggest `egf-erfc-bigprec`).

- **Option B:** Express the Berry smoothing via an incomplete-gamma representation: $\operatorname{erfc}(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2}, x^2)$. If `bigGammaIncomplete` is implementable on top of existing primitives, this is cheaper.

- **Option C (recommended for v0.1):** Use a **sharp switch** within the Stokes band, not Berry smoothing, when $|z|$ is large enough that the band is below working precision. Specifically: if $c_W |z|^{-1/(2\kappa)} < 2^{-\text{workingBits}/2}$, treat the band as zero width and refuse inputs whose $|\arg z - \theta_S|$ is in the (now-empty) band. This is conservative but deterministic.

The bead `egf` acceptance criteria should specify which option is in scope for v0.1. **Recommended:** option C, with a separate bead for full Berry smoothing.

### 6.3 The cross-platform determinism contract under `arbprec: true`

Per CLAUDE.md, the `arbprec: true` annotation guarantees **bit-identical cross-platform forever** given an explicit `--precision=<int>` standard flag. Every primitive used in the Stokes path must respect this contract:

- `cgamma`, `cdigamma` — already arbprec, bit-identical.
- `carg(z, prec)` — already arbprec (returns a `BigFloat`).
- `pFq(a; b; z, prec)` — already arbprec for $p \le q + 1$ and $|z| < 0.99$. The right-closing asymptotic uses ${}_pF_{q-1}$ evaluated at $z^{-1}$, which has $|z^{-1}| < 1$ exactly when $|z| > 1$. For $|z| < 1$ the asymptotic itself is not the right tool — fall through to the small-$z$ Slater series.
- `bigErfc` (when implemented) — must be arbprec.

**Forbidden operations in the Stokes path:**
- `Math.atan2` on float64 cast of components: **forbidden** — replaces with `carg(z, prec)`.
- `Number(z.im) > 0` predicate for sign-of-Im: **forbidden** — replaces with BigFloat comparison.
- Any `Number(...)` cast on a `BigFloat` whose value is consumed in a control-flow decision: **forbidden**.

### 6.4 Provenance fields

Per ADR-0007 / CLAUDE.md determinism callout: arbprec outputs do not write the `platform` field (no float64 involvement). The wire-schema fields `method` and `sector` are extended:

- `method`: new value `"braaksma-stokes"` for the connection-formula-aware path (algebraic + exponential terms with multipliers).
- `sector`: new value `"stokes"` for inputs in the Stokes band (between principal-interior and secondary-sector classifications).

Beyond those new labels, no new provenance fields are required by these beads.

## 9. Open mathematical questions

### 9.1 DLMF / Paris-Kaminski sign convention possible disagreement

DLMF 16.11.7 specifies $H(z e^{\mp \pi i}) + E(z)$ with $\mp = -\operatorname{sign}(\Im z)$. P&K §2.3 (around p. 80) writes the same connection with $H(z e^{\pm \pi i}) + E(z)$ using $\pm = \operatorname{sign}(\Im z)$ — but inspection of the surrounding text confirms these are the **same convention** (both authors use a "$z$ rotated to the principal value of the algebraic series argument on the appropriate side of the cut" formulation).

**Recommendation:** Trust the DLMF formula as-written, and verify against `wolframscript` at one test point in each half-plane. If the result is wrong by exactly the factor $e^{2\pi i a_h}$ for some pole $a_h$, the sign convention is flipped — invert $\operatorname{sign}_H$ and re-verify.

### 9.2 Mathematica's `MeijerG[]` principal-value convention

`MeijerG[{a-list_n, a-list_{p-n}}, {b-list_m, b-list_{q-m}}, z]` follows the DLMF parameter convention (`an` first, then `ap`; `bm` first, then `bq`). However, **Mathematica's principal-value choice when $z$ is on the branch cut** (i.e. $\arg z = \pi$ exactly) may differ from DLMF's principal-value convention for non-integer $a_h$:

- DLMF: $\arg(-1) = \pi$ (convention $\arg z \in (-\pi, \pi]$).
- Mathematica: `Arg[-1] = π` (same convention).

So principal values agree for the half-open interval. The risk is at $\arg z = -\pi$ exactly: DLMF excludes it (uses $-\pi < \arg z \le \pi$), but Mathematica may evaluate it via continuous extension from below. **Test:** evaluate at $z = -1 + i \epsilon$ for $\epsilon \in \{+10^{-30}, 0, -10^{-30}\}$; if the three values are all distinct, a half-bit of float64 disagreement in $\arg z$ near $-\pi$ would flip Mathematica's answer.

Mitigation: in the workbench code, normalise $\arg z \in (-\pi, \pi]$ exactly before sector classification — if the user supplies $z$ with $\Im z = 0, \Re z < 0$, treat $\arg z = +\pi$ (DLMF convention).

### 9.3 Edge cases not covered by DLMF

The following edge cases are mathematically real but not explicitly addressed by DLMF or P&K — the implementer must decide:

1. **Stokes line crossing at $\Im z$ exactly zero on a BigFloat scale.** If the user constructs $z$ from BigFloat arithmetic that lands $\Im z = 0$ exactly (no representation error), the input is real and the answer is real by symmetry. The Stokes-line decision becomes vacuous. **Decision:** treat real $z > 0$ as "deep principal interior" (refuse no smoothing needed); treat real $z < 0$ as on the branch cut, use DLMF's $\arg z = +\pi$ convention.

2. **Integer or half-integer pole collisions in $B_h^{\text{full}}$.** When some $a_h - a_j$ is a non-positive integer, $\Gamma(a_h - a_j)$ has a pole. The pole collisions are governed by the regularity condition of §1.2 — the input is **mathematically ill-defined**, and the code should refuse with a clear `tagged "meijer-g-asymptotic-only/parameter-collision"` rather than producing a $\pm \infty$ or NaN. This is already handled by the existing input validation (`tools/meijer-g-asymptotic-only/tool.ts`).

3. **$\kappa = 1$ and $|z|$ small.** The optimal truncation index $N^* = \lfloor |z| \rfloor$ is $0$ for $|z| < 1$, meaning the algebraic series is truncated at the first term — but the convergent ${}_pF_{q-1}$ at $z^{-1}$ (with $|z^{-1}| > 1$) is divergent. The Slater series (small-$z$ path) should be used here, not the asymptotic. **Decision:** refuse $|z| < 1$ for the asymptotic path with `non-asymptotic-regime` (already done by current code).

4. **Stokes-band width below working precision.** When $c_W |z|^{-1/(2\kappa)} < 2^{-\text{workingBits}/2}$, the band is mathematically narrower than the achievable precision and the sharp-switch implementation (option C of §6.2) becomes mandatory. **Decision:** detect this case, set band-width to zero, refuse inputs with $|\arg z - \theta_S| < 2^{-\text{workingBits}/2}$ as `tagged "meijer-g-asymptotic-only/stokes-band-below-precision"`. Document that the user needs more precision or a different path.

5. **Multiple Stokes lines at once.** For $p \le q - 2$ with $\kappa \ge 3$ and $|\arg z|$ near $\pi$ (the principal-sector edge), the input is simultaneously near the algebraic-sector boundary $\theta_{p,q}^{m,n}$ and the $E$-exponential Stokes line $\kappa \pi / 2 - 2\pi$ (if $\kappa > 2$). Both transitions are active. **Decision (v0.1):** restrict bead `egf` v0.1 scope to inputs with $|\arg z| < \theta_{p,q}^{m,n} - \pi/64$ (one safety margin away from the algebraic-sector boundary). Multi-Stokes refinement is a follow-on bead.

6. **Coalescing parameters $a_h \approx a_j$.** When two upper parameters are nearly equal (e.g. $a_1 = 1/3, a_2 = 1/3 + 10^{-20}$), the per-pole prefactor $B_h^{\text{full}}$ has $\Gamma(a_h - a_j) \sim 1/(a_h - a_j)$ — a near-pole. The cancellation between the two terms in the residue sum is exact in infinite precision but catastrophic in finite. **Decision (v0.1):** out of scope. Coalescence is tracked by the separate `coalescence.ts` path (bead hv0.10.x). Refuse inputs with $|a_h - a_j| < 2^{-\text{workingBits}/4}$ for any $h \ne j$.

7. **Vacuous parameter blocks.** The Meijer-G with $n = 0$ has vacuous `an`, so no right-closing residues exist — already refused by current code. The case $m = 0$ has vacuous `bm` and the **left-closing** residues are vacuous; this is symmetric and the asymptotic path is unaffected.

## Appendix C: Sanity check — $G^{2,0}_{0,2}$ ↔ Bessel-K

**Input:** $G^{2,0}_{0,2}(z; ; b_1, b_2)$ with $b_1 = 0$, $b_2 = 1/2$, $z = 1$.

**Closed form (Erdelyi I §5.6.4):**
$$G^{2,0}_{0,2}(z; -; b_1, b_2) = 2\, z^{(b_1 + b_2)/2}\, K_{b_1 - b_2}(2\sqrt z).$$

With $b_1 = 0, b_2 = 1/2$:
$$= 2\, z^{1/4}\, K_{-1/2}(2\sqrt z).$$

Since $K_{-1/2}(x) = K_{1/2}(x) = \sqrt{\pi/(2x)}\, e^{-x}$, at $z = 1$:
$$= 2 \cdot 1 \cdot \sqrt{\pi/4}\, e^{-2} = \sqrt{\pi}\, e^{-2}.$$

**Cross-check (research):**
```
wolframscript: N[MeijerG[{{}, {}}, {{0, 1/2}, {}}, 1], 30]
             = 0.239875543936122894736073003274
mpmath:        meijerg([[], []], [[0, 1/2], []], 1)
             = 0.239875543936122894736073003274
mpmath:        2 * 1**(1/4) * besselk(-1/2, 2)
             = 0.239875543936122894736073003274
Difference:    0.0 to 30 dps
```

This is **not directly a `43i` or `egf` benchmark** ($n = 0$ puts it out of right-closing scope) but it validates the workbench mpmath/wolframscript pipeline and confirms the closed-form identity for use in future bead hv0.9.4 (left-closing asymptotic).
