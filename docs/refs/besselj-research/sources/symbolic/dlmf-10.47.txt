Here, and throughout the remainder of
§§10.47–10.60, n is a nonnegative integer.
(This is in contrast to other treatments of spherical Bessel functions,
including Abramowitz and Stegun (1964, Chapter 10), in which n can be any
integer. However, there is a gain in symmetry, without any loss of generality
in applications, on restricting n≥0.)

Equations (10.47.1) and (10.47.2) each have a regular
singularity at z=0 with indices n, −n−1, and an irregular singularity at
z=∞ of rank 1; compare §§2.7(i)–2.7(ii).

𝗂n(1)⁡(z), 𝗂n(2)⁡(z), and 𝗄n⁡(z)
are the modified spherical Bessel functions.

Many properties of 𝗃n⁡(z), 𝗒n⁡(z),
𝗁n(1)⁡(z), 𝗁n(2)⁡(z), 𝗂n(1)⁡(z),
𝗂n(2)⁡(z), and 𝗄n⁡(z) follow straightforwardly
from the above definitions and results given in preceding sections of this
chapter. For example, z−n⁢𝗃n⁡(z), zn+1⁢𝗒n⁡(z),
zn+1⁢𝗁n(1)⁡(z), zn+1⁢𝗁n(2)⁡(z),
z−n⁢𝗂n(1)⁡(z), zn+1⁢𝗂n(2)⁡(z), and
zn+1⁢𝗄n⁡(z) are all entire functions of z.

For (10.47.1) numerically satisfactory pairs of solutions are
given by Table 10.2.1 with the symbols J, Y,
H, and ν replaced by 𝗃, 𝗒, 𝗁,
and n, respectively.

𝗂n(1)⁡(z) and 𝗄n⁡(z) in the right half of the
